    How does this work — and how far can I trust it?

    Predictability, not keywords. A language model assigns a probability to every next word. AI models write by picking high-probability words, so their output is unusually predictable to another model. People make more surprising choices — odd words, asides, uneven rhythm.

    Two published detectors, combined. Verity runs a base model and its chat-tuned sibling (Qwen2.5-0.5B) over your text and computes Fast-DetectGPT (how much more likely each word is than what the model would itself sample) and Binoculars-style perplexity ratios, plus stylometric signals like sentence-length variation.

    Calibrated percentages. A small classifier trained on 2,700 labelled human vs. GPT-3.5 / GPT-4 / Phi-3 texts turns these into a probability. Tested on writing domains it never saw, it reaches ~0.86 AUROC (≈76% accuracy at the 50% line); when it says 80–100%, it was right about 94% of the time. It is weaker on text from the newest frontier models written in a plain style.

    Highlights are per sentence. Each sentence is scored on its own tokens (borrowing a little from its neighbours). Short sentences carry little evidence, so expect them to sit near the middle.

    Limits. No AI detector is proof. Paraphrasing tools, heavy editing, very short texts, code, lists, and formulaic human writing (legal, scientific, non-native English) all cause errors. Use this as one signal — never as the sole basis for an accusation.

    Private by design. Everything runs on this machine. Your text is never sent to a third party or stored.
