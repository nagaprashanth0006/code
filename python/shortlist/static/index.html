                How is the score calculated?

                No single “ATS score” exists. Applicant tracking systems parse resumes into fields, then recruiters search, filter and sometimes rank. Each vendor and employer does this differently. Shortlist scores the parts that are knowable: how cleanly the resume parses, and how well its evidence matches a role and level.

                Reading the resume. Sections are inferred from heading formatting (styles, bold, size, capitals), fuzzy heading words (“EMPLOYMENT DETAILS”, “Educational Qualification”) and what each block contains (dates and titles → experience, degrees → education). Experience is the figure the resume states; job dates are the fallback, and “Present” is dated by the resume's own date, not today.

                Role fit = skills 55% · meaning-based similarity 20% · job titles 12% · ATS readability 13%. Each role has core (×3), important (×2) and nice-to-have (×1) skills with synonyms. A skill used in a recent job counts fully; one only listed in a skills section counts 70%.

                Position multiplies role fit by a seniority gate (40–100%) from years against the position's typical range, scope signals (led, mentored, architected, governance…) and the level of recent titles. Under 5 years of experience reduces every score (×0.88 at 4 years, ×0.64 at 2).

                Country fit is separate: stated work rights, location, language (required only where immigration rules say so, otherwise a bonus) and local CV norms. How to improve re-scores the resume with one concrete edit at a time and shows the points each would add.

                Limits. These are consistent, explainable scores, not interview odds. Templates are editable in shortlist/kb/*.json. Immigration notes are general guidance; check official sources.
